How it works

Margin and markup both describe the same profit, measured against different numbers. Margin compares profit with the selling price. Markup compares profit with the cost. Mixing them up is one of the most common quoting mistakes.

profit = price − cost margin = profit ÷ price markup = profit ÷ cost price from margin = cost ÷ (1 − margin) price from markup = cost × (1 + markup)

Examples

$1,000 cost, 30% margin

Price = 1,000 ÷ (1 − 0.30) = $1,428.57. Profit = $428.57. That is a 42.86% markup.

$1,000 cost, 30% markup

Price = 1,000 × 1.30 = $1,300.00. Profit = $300. That is only a 23.08% margin.

FAQ

Which one should I quote with?

Many businesses set prices with a target margin because it shows what share of every sales dollar is profit. Whichever you use, say which one it is so your team and suppliers read the number the same way.

Can margin be 100% or more?

No. Margin is a share of the price, so it is always below 100%. Markup has no upper limit.

Should cost include shipping and fees?

Yes, if you pay them. Use your landed cost: product price plus freight, duties and fees. The import landed cost calculator can help.
