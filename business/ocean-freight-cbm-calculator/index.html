The 1,000 kg per CBM rule and the 6,000 air divisor are common industry conventions, not government rates. Your forwarder or carrier may use different factors, minimum charges and surcharges. Confirm with their quote.

How it works

CBM = length(m) × width(m) × height(m) × packages revenue tons = max(CBM, total kg ÷ 1,000) air vol. kg = length(cm) × width(cm) × height(cm) ÷ 6,000 × packages air chargeable = max(air vol. kg, actual kg)

Inches are converted at exactly 2.54 cm, and pounds at exactly 0.45359237 kg.

Examples

Two pallets, 120 × 100 × 150 cm, 400 kg each

1.2 × 1.0 × 1.5 = 1.8 CBM per pallet, 3.6 CBM total. Weight 800 kg = 0.8 tons. Charged by volume: 3.6 W/M. By air the volumetric weight is 600 kg, so air would charge 800 kg (actual).

Ten heavy cartons, 50 × 40 × 30 cm, 120 kg each

0.06 CBM each, 0.6 CBM total. Weight 1,200 kg = 1.2 tons. Charged by weight: 1.2 W/M. At $80 per W/M the ocean freight is $96 before minimums and surcharges.

FAQ

What does W/M mean?

"Weight or measure." LCL freight is billed on the greater of the volume in CBM or the weight in metric tons. One unit of either is called a revenue ton.

When is a full container cheaper?

It depends on current rates, but as LCL volume grows, a full container can work out cheaper. Ask your forwarder to quote both when you are near the break-even point.

Should I measure the boxes or the pallet?

Measure the shipment as it will be loaded. If boxes go on pallets, use the pallet's outer size including the pallet and any overhang.
