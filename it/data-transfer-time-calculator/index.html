How it works

Internet and network speeds are quoted in megabits per second (Mbps). Files are measured in bytes. One byte is 8 bits, so 100 Mbps moves at most 12.5 megabytes per second.

seconds (theoretical) = size in bytes × 8 ÷ (Mbps × 1,000,000) seconds (estimated) = theoretical ÷ efficiency%

Real transfers are slower than the line speed because of protocol overhead, encryption, VPNs, other users on the connection and the speed of the disks at each end. Backup software that compresses or deduplicates can move less data than the full size.

Examples

2 TB first offsite backup over 100 Mbps

2 × 10¹² bytes × 8 = 1.6 × 10¹³ bits. ÷ 100,000,000 bits/s = 160,000 s = 44.4 hours at full speed. At 80% efficiency it is about 55.6 hours, or 2.3 days.

500 GB server migration over 1 Gbps

500 × 10⁹ × 8 = 4 × 10¹² bits. ÷ 1,000,000,000 = 4,000 s at full speed. At 70% efficiency: 4,000 ÷ 0.7 = 5,714 s, about 1 hour 35 minutes.

FAQ

What is the difference between Mbps and MB/s?

Mbps is megabits per second; MB/s is megabytes per second. Divide Mbps by 8 to get MB/s. A 1 Gbps link carries at most 125 MB/s.

Why does my 2 TB drive show about 1.8 TB in Windows?

The drive holds 2 × 10¹² bytes. Windows divides by 1,024⁴ and shows about 1.82, still labelled "TB". Choose TiB above if you copied the size from Windows.

Should I seed the first backup by shipping a drive?

If the estimate is several days and your connection is shared with daily work, many backup products let you copy the first full backup to a disk and ship it, then send only daily changes over the internet.
