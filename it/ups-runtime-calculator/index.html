How it works

The upper limit divides the stored energy by the load. Lead-acid batteries deliver less of their rated capacity when drained quickly, so the estimate applies Peukert's law, using the battery's 20-hour rating.

energy (Wh) = volts × batteries × Ah upper limit (h) = Wh × efficiency ÷ load current (A) = load ÷ (volts × batteries × efficiency) estimate (h) = 20 × (Ah ÷ (current × 20)) ^ Peukert

Examples

Small rack UPS, 2 × 12 V 9 Ah, 300 W load

Energy = 24 V × 9 Ah = 216 Wh. Upper limit = 216 × 0.9 ÷ 300 = 0.648 h = 38.9 min. Current = 300 ÷ (24 × 0.9) = 13.9 A. With Peukert 1.2 the estimate is about 19.6 minutes.

Same UPS, 150 W load

Halving the load more than doubles the runtime with lead-acid batteries, because they are drained more gently. Try it above: the estimate rises to roughly 45 minutes.

FAQ

VA or watts?

UPS units are rated in both. Use watts here. Keep the load below the UPS's watt rating; around 50–80% leaves headroom.

How much runtime do I need?

Enough to shut servers down cleanly, or to bridge the gap until a generator starts. For many small offices, 10–15 minutes plus automatic shutdown software is the practical goal.

When should UPS batteries be replaced?

Lead-acid UPS batteries often last 3–5 years, less in warm rooms. Many UPS units run a self-test and warn when the battery is weak.
