RAID is not a backup. It keeps the server running through a disk failure, but deletions, corruption and ransomware are copied to every disk instantly. Keep separate backups.

How it works

LevelUsable capacityMinimum drivesFailures survived
RAID 0N × size20
RAID 11 × size2N − 1
RAID 5(N − 1) × size31
RAID 6(N − 2) × size42
RAID 10N ÷ 2 × size4 (even)1 guaranteed

N is the number of drives; all drives are assumed to be the same size. RAID 10 survives one failure for certain, and more only if each failed drive is in a different mirror pair.

Examples

6 × 8 TB in RAID 6

(6 − 2) × 8 = 32 TB usable (about 29.1 TiB in Windows). Any 2 drives can fail. Efficiency 32 ÷ 48 = 67%.

4 × 4 TB in RAID 10

4 ÷ 2 × 4 = 8 TB usable. Efficiency 50%. Survives 1 failure for certain, 2 if they are in different mirror pairs.

FAQ

RAID 5 or RAID 6 for large drives?

With large drives, rebuilding after a failure can take a day or more. During that time RAID 5 has no protection left. Many admins choose RAID 6 or RAID 10 for drives of several TB.

Why is usable space in Windows lower than this result?

Windows shows sizes in TiB, and the file system and controller reserve a little space. Use the TiB figure for a closer match.

Can I mix drive sizes?

Most controllers treat every drive as the size of the smallest one. Enter the smallest drive size to be safe.
